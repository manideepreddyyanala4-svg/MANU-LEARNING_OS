## What local employers actually ask to see
ITRI E300 distinguishes preferred CV/ML study from required education and schedule; W100 distinguishes Python/annotation requirements from optional framework exposure (C019–C020). These are stronger tailoring inputs than generic lists of fashionable AI libraries. Appier tests English specification reading and testing basics; APIs and debugging tools are preferences, not a licence to list unlearned tools (C021).

Micron explicitly asks early-career candidates to show relevant academic/personal projects and be able to explain them; NVIDIA asks for role alignment; TSMC lists education, skills, language and motivation. These support showing specific evidence before broad claims of expertise (C023; S37). Role fit remains conditional on schedule, enrollment and commute.

## Recruiter context
Robert Walters Taiwan's technical CV article stresses relevant skills, restrained formatting and proofreading (C010). PERSOL Taiwan senior manager Erica Tsai recommends a brief summary and less personal information unless requested (C009). Both are attributable professional advice, not population research. Their advice does not prove that every employer rejects a resume missing a summary, photo or certain keyword.

## International-student implications
Degree enrollment and expected graduation are useful for student eligibility. The reported Thursday class and Taichung residence affect logistics but are not proof of an available number of working hours. State beginner Mandarin honestly and avoid using a polished translation to imply fluency (C015, C018, C104, C144–C145). No claim of unrestricted work eligibility is approved.

## Limits and source context
NTU's career page offers Chinese/English and sample-related training; only its index was read (S11). The videos were not watched. Some employer pages exposed no readable role text. Public samples are a purposive teaching sample, not evidence of what successful Taiwan candidates actually submitted (C040).
