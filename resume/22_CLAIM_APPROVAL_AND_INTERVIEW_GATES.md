# Claim approval and interview-defensibility gates

**Status: preparation only. No employer-facing resume is authorized yet.**

## Evidence classification

- **Primary verified:** original evidence supports a specific, precisely scoped fact.
- **Secondary supported:** credible secondary document but original unavailable.
- **User-reported:** provided by owner without independent primary confirmation.
- **Inference:** evidence-informed recommendation, not established fact.
- **Conflicted:** sources disagree; unresolved until reconciled.
- **Not verified:** insufficient evidence.
- **Superseded:** an older claim replaced by later authoritative information.

A function appearing in a repository does not prove the owner wrote it; passing automated tests does not prove performance in deployment; a professor's positive research feedback does not automatically approve external disclosure of unpublished findings.

## Before approving each possible resume claim

1. Identify **every material assertion**: action, scope, technical method, tools, numbers, dates, personal role and results.
2. Check the original record: file/commit/experiment log/transcript/publication/employer instructions; attach the exact local reference privately.
3. Verify the level of personal responsibility: designed, implemented, modified, tested, reviewed, learned, or team-only. Distinguish AI assistance from personally demonstrated understanding.
4. Check current scientific status, datasets, evaluation protocols and advisor disclosure rules.
5. Check the exact target JD: mandatory vs preferred skills, enrollment, language, hours, location and remuneration.
6. Mark the claim APPROVED, CONDITIONAL, VERIFY FIRST, or EXCLUDE, with precise reasons; do not silently promote conditional lines.
7. Prepare a technical explanation and practical follow-up questions based on the *specific chosen line*. Understanding is NOT ASSESSED until observed.
8. For final DOCX/PDF, independently check text extraction, reading order, links, page rendering and content against approved facts. No universal ATS score exists.
